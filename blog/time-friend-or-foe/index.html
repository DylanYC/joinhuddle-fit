Finding Consistency: Time — Friend or Foe?

Dylan Yip-Chuck
November 19, 2026 · 3 min read

There’s a force in the universe that tends to be either a great friend or a great foe.

Time.

We live in a fast-paced world… and yet almost every important part of our lives (our relationships, our health, our careers) is shaped by the slowness of time.

Sure, sudden and dramatic events happen. But it’s the quieter forces, thousands upon thousands of small moments and decisions, that stack and compound into the most meaningful parts of our lives.

The closeness of your friendships. The weight you can lift at the gym. The job title on your LinkedIn. No single event or decision dictates any of these.

And here’s the scary part: time doesn’t stay neutral. Left unattended, things decay. Sometimes they plateau, but more often than not, neglected things turn into problems.

~ Shift in Perspective ~

Neglected things slowly decay. But nurtured things slowly flourish, just as surely.

Time’s power can be flipped in our favour.

Take those same three:

  • Friendships. The friend you text every couple of weeks, just to check in. Years later, they’re the one who shows up when life gets hard.
  • Fitness. The “boring” routine: same three sessions a week, a little more weight each time. Two years in, you’re lifting numbers that once looked impossible.
  • Career. Twenty minutes a day on a skill nobody asked you to learn. One day, it’s the thing that sets you apart.

None of these feel like much on any given day. That’s the point.

The biggest challenge in befriending time is accepting its slow nature, and learning to embrace it.

Every technology and service in this AI age tries to deliver things faster (instantly, if possible). Our brains are recalibrating to that new normal, which makes the slowness of time… infuriating.

Patience has never been harder to come by, or more valuable. The people who have it will come out ahead.

So what does this look like day to day? Instead of leapfrogging the stepping stones, take slow, steady steps. Skip the drastic jumps to “get ahead.” Skip the clever shortcuts to beat your peers. And watch out for bursts of progress followed by long stretches of neglect.

Slow and steady wins the race. It’s a truth as old as time. If you’re nurturing the parts of your life, even in small ways, consistently, you’re making time your friend.

It’s okay to have big dreams. Just keep your routines small. Let them stack. Let them compound. Let time become your very best friend.

Which part of your life have you left unattended lately… and what’s one small thing you could do for it today?
