Finding Consistency: The Matchstick

Dylan Yip-Chuck
November 9, 2026 · 4 min read

It’s time to rethink what motivation really is.

Motivation feels like it’s this giant furnace of energy, enough to fuel us all the way to the end of the goal.

Most of us do the logical thing: we use 100% of it to fuel our work in achieving the goal.

  • GoalLose 20lbs by summer Use motivation togo to the gym
  • GoalLearn to speak a 2nd language Use motivation todownload a bunch of language apps and start crushing lessons
  • GoalWrite a book Use motivation towrite, write, write!!!

You get the picture. We tend to get tunnel vision on the obvious work which most quickly accelerates us towards the outcome we want. And if the goal is something relatively small, something like ‘waking up earlier’, we can even use the motivation to get the end result instantly: by using the energy to hop out of bed.

But we know how this story goes: the thing that felt like a never ending furnace reaches the end of its fuel supply… and goes cold. We wake up one morning and… it’s gone. No more motivation. No more energy.

What then?

More often than not, our progress comes to a halt. Twiddling our thumbs waiting for the spark to come back. And when it doesn’t, we face that horrible reality: goal = failed.

~ Shift in Perspective ~

What if instead of thinking of motivation as a fuel source, we saw it merely as the little flame on a matchstick?

Think about what you do when you light a match in real life. Your purpose is focused and singular: get this little flame somewhere QUICK… to a candle… to a stack of logs… somewhere to outlast its inevitable demise!

Let’s replay those goals, this time, with the matchstick perspective in mind.

  • GoalLose 20lbs by summer Use motivation torid the house of temptations, build a well thought out plan which keeps things fun and doable without being too ambitious, tell a close friend and ask them to check in on your progress, and so on and so forth…
  • GoalLearn to speak a 2nd language Use motivation toresearch different learning methods and add a few to the mix that suit your learning style best, seek out a friend who’s also learning the language and set up calls on weekends to share progress and practice, pick a future date and circle it on the calendar when you want to take the level A1 test and give yourself a reward if you pass it, and so on and so forth…
  • GoalWrite a book Use motivation toclean your workspace, add a few blocks of time to your calendar for uninterrupted work time, and so on and so forth…

Hopefully you get the picture. There’s a million and one ways to help yourself succeed that aren’t actually “progressing” the goal itself in the direct sense.

Warning

Does this mean you neglect the actual work and just procrastinate with peripheral things that don’t actually lead to real progress?

It can if you’re not careful. This peripheral work can actually feel a lot like “real” work and give you a sense of accomplishment when you haven’t really done anything.

The right approach is a balanced one. Harness the motivation in part to get to work: to start building momentum and to start getting reps in… but not all of it. Some of it, however much is necessary, should be spent on building the system to fall back on once the motivation runs cold.

And even once you’re in the swing of things, and the initial systems you built are working… you can never stop stoking the fire and going out to chop wood to keep it going.

When you’re basking in motivation and energy, ask yourself:

“When I wake up one day, and do not feel like doing any work, what’s going to keep me going?”
