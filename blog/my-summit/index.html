Consistency & Potential

My Summit Public Launch!

Dylan Yip-Chuck
November 24, 2026 · 5 min read

Is there anything better in this world than reaching the summit after a long and winding journey?

After many years, many late nights, and many early mornings, my passion project, Risers, is finally released to the public in 175 countries.

This is one of the proudest and happiest achievements of my life. If not, the greatest one.

I know… it’s just an app. And I know, there’s millions of these little apps floating around out there. And millions more coming with the Claude and the ChatGPT AIs of the world now at everyone’s fingertips… enabling people like me to build without writing a single line of code.

But, this little app, weighing only 165MB of space on your phone, is much bigger to me.

This wasn’t a weekend hack job. This isn’t one of those “I vibe coded an app in 48 hours” stories. This has been years in the making. Years of slowly thinking about the problem of consistency. Years of failing, over and over. And years of slowly tinkering away on wireframes and app names: “GymBuddies”, “FitBuddies”, “FitBuds”, “Huddle”, and now, Risers.

Years of almost giving up.

And if my Risers story was stripped down to a single line, it would be:

“I finally never gave up.”

You see, my pattern prior to this was a guy who gave up. A guy who started projects but never finished them. A guy who dreamed more than he actually accomplished. This was becoming my self-talk track. My self identity was cementing more and more each year, that I was a quitter.

If we really dig it all up, the origins of this self identity go back to my late teenage years, when I gave up on my dream to play pro hockey. At 17, I watched my best friends and teammates get drafted to the Ontario Hockey League. I was 1 of only 3 others on my team who went undrafted. This nearly killed me!

In the hockey community, I was labelled as “injury prone” and outside onlookers gave the crutch that it was my femur break or my shoulder separations which was why it ended for me. But, I know the real reason: it wasn’t my injuries… I failed because I gave up. I didn’t consistently do the rehab I knew I needed to do to get healthy and back on the ice. And it was very much possible to. My long-time defence partner of 7 seasons, for example, had the identical shoulder injury as I did, and he went on to fully recover and go pro.

(Quick note: I am not saying I would have gone pro… don’t worry, I am not one of those “I’d have gone pro if only…X, Y, Z” guys lol! I never had the same God given talent as my friends who made it. But I certainly know I quit before my potential. I know that.)

Consistency and discipline have never been in my nature. On the flip side, it’s in my nature to dream big and be hyper motivated in short spurts. My issue is doing the work you know you have to do when you don’t feel like doing it. This is why I failed as a hockey player at 17. And it’s the same reason why, since then, I’ve never really accomplished anything of real significance. It wasn’t for lack of ideas, or motivation. Those have been plentiful.

I just suck at the consistency part. And in the end, without consistency, all roads end before the summit…

So, maybe now, you can see why this little app, Risers, means so much to me. On many levels, it liberated me from the horrible pattern I’d entrenched for myself since I was 17 years old. Now at 30, I finally made it to the summit on one of my big dreams after a very long and winding road. Many moments I wanted to quit. Many moments it would have been easier to fall into the grooves of my old ways and give up when things got hard.

You’ll find a lot of this story tucked quietly inside of Risers. In the name, in the visuals, in the monthly consistency challenge (which I call “The Climb”). My story is deeply embedded within this thing.

To end this little blog, I thought I’d share my unedited note from my phone that I wrote at 2am last night, as I couldn’t fall asleep. I was too busy dreaming! Something I can’t help but do…

We all have something in our life (or maybe even multiple things!) that we wish we were doing more of to be healthier. Whether it’s an ambition to be more active, or perhaps a desire to eat better, or to read more, or to sleep longer, or perhaps even to put more time into a hobby like playing an instrument…or painting…or knitting or whatever it is you’d love to do. And yet, so often we attempt to stay on top of these things completely alone with just our own individual discipline and will power to rely on.

I’ve always struggled with these things: discipline and will power. I so badly want to be consistent, I know consistency is the key. But, I’m the kind of person to be hyper motivated at the start, and then drop off a massive cliff after a bit of time…after the motivation fades.

I realized that for me, there was a formula I was missing. This formula is: daily plans, actually written down, plus witnesses, actually aware of the plan and whether I followed through.

So, I built risers to give this formula a home. Its purpose is to help people stick with whatever they’re trying to stick with, with their partner or inner circle by their side.

You join with your circle (a partner, a few close friends, or a few family members), make and share your own personal daily plans of whatever you’re wanting to stick with (however big or small), snap a quick photo or video as proof, and share these little moments and wins throughout the day.

It turns out this little act, simply sharing healthy moments with that person or those few who really have your back is also an amazing way to stay in touch from anywhere; to stay in contact in a simple, unforced and healthy way. Most apps on our phones these days…most technology in general… tends to do more harm than good for our health. And tend to prioritize seeing strangers. Risers is meant to flip that upside down: to put your health and your closest people back in the spotlight.

I hope you try it with your circle. And I hope it helps you find a bit more consistency with whatever it is in your life which would help you feel healthy. And I hope this becomes a space for you to connect even more… and keep each other going!

A footnote on Risers

Risers, perhaps not so ironically, is about helping people like myself crack the code on being more consistent. Plan(s) + witness(es) is my formula for consistency and I hope that it can help others like me too.

— Dylan
